# Participant-controlled availability and communication audit — 0.3.1-dev

## Mechanisms actually implemented

- `policy.py`: every participant receives a frozen `Observation`, including only their own current outage history, availability status, private relocation preference, received messages, source exposure counts, resources and pending requests. The script's relocation/return decisions are made here. `Action('relocate')` is allowed even without a blackout for replacement policies; the scripted baseline uses `payload='while_unpowered'` to expire a deferred order upon recovery. The script calls `Action('review')` to ask for a later observation near its own selected preference threshold. Six restored hours before scripted return is a C assumption. The engine does **not** decide either move.
- `engine.py`: sends a policy notification on an affected residence's new outage or an away household's restoration; validates requests, records requested/accepted/deferred/executed/completed stages, allocates real simulated road capacity to travel, supports cancellation before departure and limits local activities while temporarily away. Outside-region resources are an unresolved boundary, not guaranteed accommodation. The same world runs with a policy returning `Action('none')` and produces no relocations.
- `network.py`/`engine.py`: separate close, workplace, community and online contacts, institutional feed; message origin, evidence, propagation delay, recipient access/attention and forwarding limited by TTL. Messages in the inbox are delivered, not omniscient. Repeated forwards from the same origin do not create independent adoption exposures. Claim counts persist (forgetting unmodeled).
- **Baseline decision actually altered by messages:** two independent observed stockout sources for a participant with threshold two increase grocery target from 2.5 to 4 days of need; if cash, stock or roads are unavailable, the corresponding attempted quantity may not be fulfilled. An integration regression test verifies that two *delivered* message records change the requested quantity from 2.5 to 4 units for a household with one unit/day need.
- **Not currently caused by peers:** default relocation, medication consumption, payment choices, commute, return decision, store selection, and clinic visits. A replacement policy *may* use messages for any of these, but there is no source-derived quantitative coefficient for that effect. Delivered `power_interruption` announcements do not override the household's own power status or independently force a move.

## Evidence boundaries

1. Centola, D. (2010). The spread of behavior in an online social network experiment. *Science, 329*(5996), 1194–1197. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1185231 — randomized structured network experiment, online health-tool uptake about 54% clustered versus 38% randomized groups. Supports multiple-source behavioral reinforcement **as a possible mechanism**, not the synthetic source thresholds (25/55/20%), message transmission/attention probabilities, or an effect on stockouts or relocation.
2. Sadus, K., Göttmann, J., & Schubert, A.-L. (2023). Predictors of stockpiling behavior during the COVID-19 pandemic in Germany. *Journal of Public Health, 31*, 1717–1733. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10389-022-01727-x — online survey n=861; descriptive social norm beliefs associated with self-reported stockpiling. Provides **domain-relevant associative context** but neither an identified causal coefficient nor a valid transfer to regional utility disruption. Not used for numerical fitting.
3. Sue Wing, I., et al. (2025). A method to estimate the economy-wide consequences of widespread, long duration electric power interruptions. *Nature Communications, 16*, 3335. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-58537-4 — stated hypothetical relocation preference 33% one-day and 83% fourteen-day, presented with utility notification of *prospective* outage duration. Simulator infers thresholds from *elapsed* outage hours instead: this translation is a C-level extrapolation. The source does not identify influence of neighbor messages on relocation or actual destination capacity.

## Version-specific three-seed fitted-point checks

`python -m scripts.validate_availability`, household sample n=2400, retailer sample n=120, seeds 41–43, complete regional interruption:

- One-day fraction temporarily away: 0.32569, min 0.31667, max 0.33042; survey stated-response anchor 0.33.
- Fourteen-day fraction temporarily away: 0.82972, min 0.81875, max 0.83667; survey stated-response anchor 0.83.
- 49% business shutdown preference transferred to retailer power operability is still only a provisional contextual proxy.
- All relocated households returned by the end of these scenarios; this does not establish a calibrated return-time distribution.
- Software tests include no-move policy, alternative move policy, no forced relocation from power messages, genuine two-message purchase difference, cancellation of deferred transport, and invariant checks.

**Gaps:** actual relocation actions and costs, participant observation timing, alternatives to relocation, outside-region lodging and supplies, destination capacity, social-network topology and temporal contact rates, individual communication-to-behavior causal effects, conditional correlations with income/support network, forecasted restoration messages, belief updating/forgetting. No independent historical behavior validation. Prior `reports/*` output artifacts were produced by earlier builds unless explicitly regenerated.
