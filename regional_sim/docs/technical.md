# Technical model and methods (0.1.0)

## Geographic and temporal definition

Target geography: Columbus, OH metropolitan statistical area, CBSA 18140; synthetic zones subdivide the same nominal boundary. 2024 ACS one-year published population parameters anchor only median income and geography; no ACS PUMS joint-distribution calibration has been performed. Synthetic start April 1, 2024, 00:00 local Eastern clock. Retail and selected essential-provider employees have independently determined rotating weekend shifts. Scripted shoppers can schedule intended travel for the next advertised opening rather than assuming 24-hour shops. All events are time-stamped in fractional hours. This is an illustrative 2024 reference simulation, not a historical digital twin. 2025 regulatory citations are *context*, not automatically applied retroactively.

The event scheduler is a stable `(time, insertion_sequence, kind, payload)` heap. Each individual receives an initially independent decision time in hours [5,20], then a 6-to-36-hour bounded exponentially distributed decision interval (mean generator scale 20 hours), and independently delayed requested actions. Power and essential service balances advance hourly; procurement every six hours; production, biological use, billing and attendance reset daily; payroll weekly. These *physical and institutional clocks* do not synchronize participant decision times. Travel uses capacity reservations and BPR departure-time travel calculation. Some normal operations such as checkout are instantaneous at arrival with separately delayed settlement.

## Conserved quantities

Deposits and cash are discrete integer cents. Transaction authorizations debit the sender and place funds in a separately accounted escrow; settlement moves them to receivers. Thus:

`initial monetary assets = sum(account deposits) + sum(physical cash accounts) + outstanding payment escrow`.

Bank reserves and the synthetic collateral facility are a separate conserved reserve pool: `R0 = reserve_bank_0 + reserve_bank_1 + reserve_facility`; a liquidity advance credits bank reserves while reducing facility reserves and increasing its recorded debt. The facility is **not** a representation of current Federal Reserve eligible collateral, pricing or intraday lending rules. Deposits are never created from payroll or a fictitious external grant. External employment is represented by an initially capitalized outside-region counterparty account.

Grocery bundles and abstract medication units have distinct account-owner locations: each household; each retailer; warehouse; upstream supplier; clinic; freight in transit; consumed and spoiled ledgers. The global boundary-production event is a *documented goods source*, so `initial goods + cumulative boundary production = goods in all owners' stocks + freight in transit + cumulative consumption + cumulative spoilage`. Transactions may change ownership only after payment authorization, and goods in transit move only on an actual successful road-reservation arrival. No supplier has unlimited instantaneous goods. Boundary raw material use and mass conversion are not modeled.

Energy is hourly kWh (one-hour average kW); `supply/import energy = energy served + unused/spilled available energy` and `consumer demand = served + unserved`. There is **no inter-hour storage** or synchronous AC grid dynamics; unmet energy is not a negative physical energy stock. Clinic medication consumed on appointment, and water demand is measured in cubic meters; never infer health outcomes from these quantities.

## Six ordinary operating domains

1. **Finance/payments** — heterogeneous household, merchant, warehouse, supplier, utility, clinic, government and regional-external deposit accounts; individual cash holdings; recurring utility payments and weekly payroll; same-bank postings, POS-like authorization/escrow, a simplified next-business-day 08:30 Eastern ACH-like posting, infrastructure-dependent authorization and a bank reserve/liquidity queue with explicitly accounted synthetic collateral advances. FedACH provides a real schedule concept, but the synthetic POS and simplified batch logic do *not* implement full network or Regulation CC compliance. US federal holidays are not implemented. Interbank reserve deficits cannot settle without reserve or authorized facility transfers.
2. **Physical retail/supply** — two separately accounted goods, household ownership and consumption, store stock and opening hours, threshold reorder procedures, prepaid procurement, warehouse and supplier inventory, boundary production, truck shipments with bounded batch size and scheduled road capacity, backorders via deferrals, recorded household stockout observations and loss of power-dependent refrigerated inventory after a synthetic duration. Pharmacy distribution is abstract; clinic med inventory can be replenished.
3. **Electricity** — aggregate hourly zonal feeder caps, local critical load, noncritical residential/merchant load, external/regional supply input and ordinary/seasonal load profiles. Supply is allocated to segregated critical demand before flexible/noncritical load, with explicit curtailed load and temporary communications backup. This is a constrained scalar energy-balance **approximation**, not a validated load-flow, feeder relay, voltage, frequency, redispatch, UFLS or UVLS model. NERC EOP-011-4 R1.2.5 motivates protecting designated critical-load circuits, but neither the modeled abstraction nor that post-reference-period standard is claimed as an operative Columbus-specific procedure.
4. **Mobility/logistics** — a zonal synthetic road corridor whose real hour-slot reservations are shared by simulated household trips and goods-truck units. Travel time `t = t0[1+0.15(v/c)^4]`; `t0 = 0.4 h`, conventional FHWA/BPR coefficient use. Reservations above available capacity are refused and retried. Historical Columbus route-level assignment, transit/taxi share, multi-link route selection, accidents and vehicle conservation are not calibrated or modeled. A person trip consumes a vehicle-equivalent; its unit is deliberately explicit.
5. **Communication** — array-backed graph with clustered household, coworker, local-community and public-online directed ties; geographically gated official notices and delayed secondary news delivery; personal digital/media access, attention, trust and delivery delay. Message IDs preserve original source IDs across forwarding, observed/event-time provenance, evidence type, timestamp, channel, source visibility and recipient lists. Claims can be on-site stockouts, first-hand power loss, purchase intentions and actual purchases, questions, agreement/disagreement, authentic power announcements or forwarded previously delivered claims. Fabricated firsthand claims cannot be sent through the participant action API. A question/opinion is not treated as independent stockout evidence. Observed distinct independent sources are counted separately from repeats/forwarding; 1/2/3-source synthetic thresholds distinguish simple from reinforced adoption. The mechanism follows Centola's *domain-specific experimental distinction*, not an import of its adoption effect sizes.
6. **Essential services** — aggregate water availability (m³/hour) from critical electric service, plus clinic FIFO appointment capacity affected by available electricity, personnel attendance and medication stock. Pending appointments are unique by household, with a policy revisit interval. This is a service-continuity and resource-use model, not a medical clinical-triage model. No morbidity or deaths are generated. Local facility staffing and hydraulic protocols remain unverified.

## Ordinary cross-domain causal dependencies

- Household requests and prices -> funds authorization -> retailer transfers owned goods; payment outages can prompt cash fallback.
- Inventory thresholds + staff + working capital -> prepaid physical shipments -> freight vehicle reservation -> receipt at destination; no teleportation of restocks.
- Feeder and regional supply -> local shop power, refrigeration loss (explicit spoilage), protected communications and essential capacity; bank connectivity is also channel constrained.
- Employment attendance depends on completed physical commute. Stores can be understaffed and clinics have workforce-limited capacity. External payroll counterparty is represented explicitly.
- Household need and price information -> actions; stockout observation or experienced power loss -> access-gated messages -> repeated exposure -> optional change in desired groceries. Information sources are local to the participant or attributable to an institution.
- Power interruptions cause zone-specific institutional announcements; secondary news repeats the same institution as a *single source*, so it does not artificially increase independent reinforcement.

`--disable-coupling` uses flags `power_to_retail`, `power_to_services`, `power_to_communications`, `power_to_payments`, `supply_to_retail`, `workforce_to_retail`, `workforce_to_supply` for negative controls. This is a deliberate research instrumentation switch, not an ordinary safeguard bypass in default scenarios; test results with ablations are explicitly labeled non-realistic controls.

## Replaceable action and observation contract

The `Policy` protocol `decide(observation: Observation, rng: random.Random) -> list[Action]` returns a set of intended actions; every observation is a frozen dataclass of the actor's own accounts/resources, local power, previously queried or physically observed retailer stock, independently delivered messages and pending action references. The policy receives no `World`, other household accounts, live retailer database or all-zone factor arrays. `MessageView` is frozen and contains a claim, provenance, channel, credibility and visibility. The policy instance can of course be shared across actors; **plugin authors must not introduce a cross-agent information leak through their own mutable policy state**. Testing the interface does not sandbox untrusted arbitrary Python code.

`Action(kind, amount=0, target=-1, payload='', delay_hours=0, reference=-1)` supports `buy` (grocery bundles) / `buy_medicine` (medication units, locally assigned store), `commute`, `pay_bill` (dollars), `clinic`, `query` (`store_stock`), `send` (verified firsthand `power_interruption`/`retail_stockout`), `forward` (delivered message ID, max TTL two), `ask` (`retail_stockout` or `power_interruption`), `agree`/`disagree` (received message ID), `intended_message`/`completed_message` (`buy`, only recent legitimate intent or sale), `schedule` (ordinary `buy`/`buy_medicine`/`clinic`/`query` in up to 168 hours), `cancel` (same-agent, still-requested ID), `none`. A participant may emit any list of available legal actions concurrently; no one-domain-per-agent restriction. Rejected actions are logged and cannot move resources. Requests, acceptance, execution and completion are distinct trace stages. Timing fields are not claims of response-time estimates.

## Empirical and protocol grounding

Each item in `provenance.json` has name, units, full bibliographic title/URL where evidence exists, A/B/C classification, estimate/distribution, applicability, transformation, uncertainty, validation target, and limitation. Class A applies only to direct reported values/experimental mechanisms, B to documented methods or protocols, and C to synthetic distributions and extrapolations. Crucially, anchoring a C synthetic distribution to a local A median does not validate its joint distribution or behavioral response. EIA's 861 kWh/customer/month is a **US** 2023 benchmark, not a Columbus-specific fit. Centola (2010) tested adoption of an online health tool and its effect magnitudes do not generalize to retail shortages.

## Limits on institutional validity and planned calibration

Absent: named banks, deposits/check hold application of 12 CFR Part 229, NACHA settlement/returns/calendar; feeder topology, certified IEEE/NERC relay timing and power-flow verification; freight route GIS and public transit; clinical demand/risk distributions and physician triage; firm balance-sheet/production input-output tables; source-calibrated shopping and communication policies; fully independent held-out disturbance comparisons. These are *required* for an empirical release. Preserve and report them rather than silently adding precision to synthetic parameters.


## 0.2.0-dev source-linked calibration extension

The generic historical 0.1.0 description above retains its original 24-minute free-flow default. `Config.free_flow_minutes` / `--free-flow-minutes` now replace this fixed default when supplied (the opt-in `configs/calibrated_2024.json` fits about 23.7693 minutes to a *proxy* for the ACS 24.1-minute commuter mean).

Electricity retains the hourly scalar energy calculation but now records three additional diagnostic counters: last-hour count of household fixed-power ranks exceeding the supplied zonal service ratio, maximum such count and cumulative unserved household-hours. `power_hourly` stores time/count/zone-averaged noncritical ratio for reproducible diagnostics. These are **proxy reported customer interruption counts**; no empirical feeder/circuit topology or exact household meter voltage states are modeled. Shock/restore events take precedence over hourly domain events when their scheduled time is identical. At an externally specified shock end, the model does not simulate physical crew repair. See `docs/calibration.md` for the unidentifiable AEP-to-ACS account expansion, fitted factors, observed residuals and per-domain remaining gaps. All original monetary/physical conservation rules and critical-load allocation are unchanged.

## Reversible operational availability extension (0.3.0-dev; revised 0.3.1-dev)

In 0.3.1-dev, the world no longer chooses participant relocation or return: it updates private outage/restoration observations and notifies policies on service changes. Participants request `relocate`, `return`, `review` and `cancel`; engine enforces availability, transport and request-stage constraints. See `docs/participant_communication.md`. Old calibration figures in archived reports belong to previous engine behavior.
See `docs/operational_availability.md` for state definitions, nonmedical source-linked fit, conditional utility dependencies, conservation treatment, validation and uncertainty. The default is enabled; `--no-availability` is a negative-control ablation. The calibration corresponds to stated scenario preferences and is not independent historical action validation.
